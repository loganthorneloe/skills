# Source audit records (accessed 2026-11-03)

### mRNA vaccines — a new era in vaccinology
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd.2017.243; text read from: PMC author manuscript https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5906799/
- byline: Norbert Pardi, Michael J. Hogan, Frederick W. Porter, Drew Weissman · review article · Nature Reviews Drug Discovery · January 2018
- verdicts: C2 conditional (general LNP description only; predates the COVID vaccines, so use for platform features not product specifics); C3 pass; C4 pass (mechanism framing; review notes TFH/GC responses "remain poorly understood" in 2018); C8 conditional (states mRNA is "non-integrating" and degraded by normal processes as a platform property; not a measurement in vaccinated people)
- use: Introduction (safety, efficacy paragraphs), "Modulation of immunogenicity", LNP paragraph in delivery section, Figure 1, Figure 3
- credibility: Pardi and Weissman run the University of Pennsylvania lab that developed nucleoside-modified mRNA–LNP vaccines; Weissman shared the 2023 Nobel Prize for this work. A peer-reviewed review in a major journal.
- contribution: Summarises how IVT mRNA is built (cap, UTRs, poly(A), modified nucleosides), how innate sensors detect it, LNP composition (~100 nm, ionizable lipid enabling endosomal release, PEG-lipid, cholesterol, phospholipid), and what drives vaccine effectiveness (APC expression, DC maturation/migration, TFH and GC B cells).
- limits: Pre-pandemic (2018); authors have patents/financial interests in mRNA technology (review discloses competing interests); "non-integrating" is a mechanistic statement, not a genome-sequencing study.
- read: partial (full text extracted; read intro, immunogenicity, delivery, safety, all figure captions; skimmed cancer-vaccine sections) · author manuscript via PMC
- retraction check: none known; one search for corrections not run separately (covered by general searches; none surfaced).

### Safety and Efficacy of the BNT162b2 mRNA Covid-19 Vaccine
- url: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2034577; text read from: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7745181/
- byline: Fernando P. Polack, Stephen J. Thomas, and colleagues (C4591001 Clinical Trial Group) · randomized controlled trial · New England Journal of Medicine · December 2020
- verdicts: C2 pass (product description: LNP, nucleoside-modified RNA, full-length prefusion-stabilized spike); C4 conditional (cites its own phase 1 results for neutralizing antibodies and CD8+/CD4+ T cells; not measured in this paper); C6 pass
- use: Abstract; Introduction; Efficacy section; Table 2; Figure 3; limitations paragraph in Discussion
- credibility: The pivotal phase 3 trial: 43,548 people randomized 1:1 to vaccine or saline placebo, observer-blinded, cases confirmed by PCR. Primary data, regulatory-grade.
- contribution: 8 vs 162 cases ≥7 days after dose 2 among participants without prior infection → 95.0% efficacy (95% credible interval 90.3–97.6); 10 severe cases after dose 1, 9 placebo vs 1 vaccine; Figure 3 shows the curves separating ~12 days after dose 1.
- limits: Sponsored and partly authored by Pfizer/BioNTech; median ~2 months of follow-up after dose 2; ancestral-strain era before Delta/Omicron; endpoint is symptomatic disease, not infection or transmission; too small for rare adverse events (authors' own statement).
- read: partial (abstract, intro, methods summary, results, discussion, figure captions) · via PMC
- retraction check: none (well-known, no correction affecting efficacy numbers surfaced).

### Immune imprinting, breadth of variant recognition, and germinal center response in human SARS-CoV-2 infection and vaccination
- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2022.01.018; text read from: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8786601/
- byline: Katharina Röltgen, Sandra C. A. Nielsen, and colleagues (senior author Scott D. Boyd) · research article · Cell · January 2022
- verdicts: C4 pass (germinal centres in vaccinee lymph nodes intact with spike present); C5 pass (direct tissue measurement)
- use: Results section on LN localization; Figure 7 (A–E); limitations paragraph
- credibility: Stanford pathology group that examined actual lymph node biopsies from vaccinated people with RNA in situ hybridization probes specific to vaccine mRNA and spike immunostaining; peer-reviewed in Cell.
- contribution: Vaccine mRNA found in germinal centres of axillary lymph nodes on days 7, 16, 37 after dose 2, lower but appreciable at day 60; spike antigen abundant at day 16 and present at day 60. Non-vaccinee biopsies negative.
- limits: Small numbers (seven vaccinees in histology comparisons), biopsies not at prospective preset time points (taken for clinical reasons); shows presence in lymph nodes, not elsewhere and not effects on DNA.
- read: partial (summary, LN results, Figure 6–7 captions, limitations) · via PMC
- retraction check: one search; no erratum found.

### Press release: The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2023
- url: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2023/press-release/
- byline: The Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet · prize announcement · nobelprize.org · 2 October 2023
- verdicts: C1 pass (plain description of DNA→mRNA→protein); C3 pass (summary of 2005/2008/2010 findings); C6 conditional (repeats ~95% figure; use primary trial instead)
- use: sections "mRNA vaccines: A promising idea", "The breakthrough", Figure 2
- credibility: The expert committee that reviewed the evidence to award the prize; its summary cites the primary papers (Karikó et al. 2005 Immunity, 2008 Mol Ther, 2010 NAR).
- contribution: Explains that unmodified in vitro transcribed mRNA triggered inflammation in dendritic cells, and that base modifications nearly abolished it and increased protein production.
- limits: Secondary summary written for a general audience; celebratory; not a methods source.
- read: full · accessed 2026-11-03

### Duration of effectiveness of vaccines against SARS-CoV-2 infection and COVID-19 disease: results of a systematic review and meta-regression
- url: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(22)00152-0; text read from: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8863502/
- byline: Daniel R. Feikin, Melissa M. Higdon, and colleagues · systematic review and meta-regression · The Lancet · February 2022
- verdicts: C7 pass (for the pre-Omicron period, vaccines including both mRNA products)
- use: Summary/Findings; Figure 2; Discussion of bias
- credibility: WHO-led team pooling 18 trials and observational studies (78 estimates) with formal risk-of-bias assessment.
- contribution: From 1 to 6 months after full vaccination, effectiveness fell ~21 points against infection, ~25 against symptomatic disease, ~10 against severe disease; 81% of severe-disease estimates stayed above 70%.
- limits: All studies before Omicron spread; seven of 18 studies at serious risk of bias; waning partly may reflect bias; article was later corrected (correction notice on PMC; not examined in detail, numbers quoted from current PMC version).
- read: partial (summary, figure captions, bias paragraph) · via PMC

### Intracellular Reverse Transcription of Pfizer BioNTech COVID-19 mRNA Vaccine BNT162b2 In Vitro in Human Liver Cell Line
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/cimb44030073; text read from: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8946961/ (MDPI blocked automated fetch)
- byline: Markus Aldén, Francisko Olofsson Falla, and colleagues (Lund University) · in vitro research article · Current Issues in Molecular Biology (MDPI) · February 2022
- verdicts: C9 conditional (use only as a report of what was seen in one cancer cell line in a dish, with the authors' own statement that integration was not shown)
- use: Abstract; Methods 2.1; Discussion (integration sentence, Huh7 limitation)
- credibility: Primary lab data from a university group; small journal; findings never shown in people.
- contribution: Huh7 liver carcinoma cells exposed to 0.5–2 µg/mL BNT162b2 for 6–48 h; PCR on genomic DNA amplified vaccine sequence; LINE-1 expression changed. Authors: "At this stage, we do not know if DNA reverse transcribed from BNT162b2 is integrated into the cell genome."
- limits: Cancer cell line with active replication and high LINE-1 activity; doses argued to exceed in vivo; no in vivo or genome-sequencing data; critiqued in a published comment (Merchant 2022). No retraction or correction found in one search.
- read: partial (abstract, methods, discussion) · via PMC

### Comment on Aldén et al. ... (Curr. Issues Mol. Biol. 2022, 44, 1115–1126)
- url: https://doi.org/10.3390/cimb44040113; text read from: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9164063/
- byline: Hamid A. Merchant · letter/comment · Current Issues in Molecular Biology (MDPI) · April 2022
- verdicts: C9 conditional (expert critique, argument not new data)
- use: main text points 1–3
- credibility: Pharmaceutics academic (University of Huddersfield) arguing from pharmacokinetics; published in the same journal alongside the paper.
- contribution: Argues Huh7 in vitro model does not reflect living tissue, the doses exceed in vivo exposure (EMA distribution data refer to lipids, not mRNA), and transfected cells would be cleared by the immune response.
- limits: Opinion, no experiments.
- read: partial (main argument)

## Gaps
- C8: only conditional sources — no audited human genome-sequencing study showing absence of integration; the claim rests on established molecular biology (textbook) plus a review's platform statement. Reported as a limit.
- C4: human T-cell data cited only indirectly (Polack citing phase 1); germinal-centre evidence direct (Röltgen).
- Disputed premise: popular claims that vaccine mRNA is gone "in a few days" are too strong for lymph nodes (C5).
- Out of scope: safety profile (e.g. myocarditis) not audited.
