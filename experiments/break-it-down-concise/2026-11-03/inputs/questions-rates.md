# Coverage questions — interest rates and inflation (frozen before generation)
Score each 0/1/2 from the report alone. 2 = a novice reading only this report could answer correctly; 1 = partial, implicit, or needs inference the report does not support; 0 = missing or wrong.
Q1. What is inflation and what does the central bank actually set? Key: a sustained rise in the general price level; the bank sets a short-term policy rate (e.g. federal funds target) that influences other rates.
Q2. How does a higher policy rate reach households and businesses? Key: borrowing costs rise for loans, mortgages, credit; saving becomes more attractive; borrowing and spending fall.
Q3. Why does less spending lower inflation? Key: lower demand relative to what the economy can produce reduces pressure for firms to raise prices and wages; slack in labor markets slows wage growth.
Q4. Name at least two other channels. Key: asset prices/wealth (stocks, housing fall), exchange rate (currency strengthens, imports cheaper), credit availability, expectations.
Q5. Why do expectations matter? Key: if people expect high inflation they set wages/prices accordingly; credible policy anchors expectations, making inflation easier to lower.
Q6. Why does it take so long? Key: long and variable lags; fixed-rate debt and contracts adjust slowly; spending decisions and hiring change gradually; estimates of a year or more for peak effect on inflation.
Q7. What is the cost or risk? Key: slower growth and higher unemployment, possible recession; overtightening risk because of lags; doesn't fix supply shocks directly.
Q8 (transfer). Inflation rises because a war cuts oil supply. What would raising rates do and not do? Key: it can't increase oil supply; it reduces demand elsewhere and may prevent the shock from spreading into expectations/wages, at the cost of weaker output.
