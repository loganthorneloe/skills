# Coverage questions — causal inference (frozen before generation)

Score each 0/1/2 from the report alone. 2 = a novice reading only this report could answer correctly; 1 = partial, implicit, or needs inference the report does not support; 0 = missing or wrong.

Q1. Why does "app users score higher" fail to show the app helped?
Key: users and non-users can differ before using the app (motivation, prior ability, time, resources); the comparison mixes the app's effect with those pre-existing differences (selection).

Q2. What is the counterfactual (potential) outcome, and why is it the core difficulty?
Key: the effect for a person is the difference between their score with the app and the score the same person would have had without it; only one is ever observed, so effects must be estimated by comparing groups.

Q3. What is a confounder? Give an example relevant to the app.
Key: a factor that influences both who uses the app and exam scores (e.g. motivation, prior grades, parental support), so it can create a gap with no app effect.

Q4. How does random assignment solve the problem?
Key: chance, not the person, decides who gets the app, so groups are alike on average in measured and unmeasured traits; a remaining systematic difference is attributable to the app (plus chance variation).

Q5. How do you tell a real difference from chance?
Key: random groups differ by chance; larger groups make chance gaps smaller; use uncertainty (spread/standard error/confidence interval or significance) to judge whether the observed gap exceeds what chance alone would produce.

Q6. What does random assignment guarantee versus random/representative sampling?
Key: assignment makes the comparison fair inside the study (internal validity); it does not make the participants representative of the wider population (generalization/external validity), which needs sampling or replication.

Q7. If random assignment is impossible, what can you do and what is the catch?
Key: compare similar people / adjust for measured confounders, or use natural or quasi-experiments (before/after with comparison, discontinuities, etc.); conclusions rest on assumptions that unmeasured differences do not explain the gap.

Q8 (transfer). A district gives the app to classes whose teachers volunteered; those classes score 8 points higher than the others. Name two non-app explanations and a better design.
Key: volunteer teachers may be more motivated/skilled, classes may differ in prior achievement or resources; better design is to randomly assign classes or teachers (or at least compare prior scores / similar classes), with enough classes for chance to average out.
