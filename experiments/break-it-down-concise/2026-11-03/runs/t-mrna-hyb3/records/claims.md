# Claims
- C1: mRNA is a short-lived working copy of a gene that ribosomes in the cytoplasm read to build a protein (textbook background).
- C2: The COVID-19 mRNA vaccines encode the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein (BNT162b2: full-length, membrane-anchored, two-proline prefusion-stabilized), packaged in lipid nanoparticles (~100 nm, four lipid components) that carry mRNA into cells and release it into the cytoplasm.
- C3: Chemically modified nucleosides (pseudouridine, 1-methylpseudouridine) reduce innate immune sensing of injected mRNA and increase protein production.
- C4: Spike made from vaccine mRNA trains the adaptive immune system: antigen-presenting cells (dendritic cells) carry/express antigen, migrate to lymph nodes, and drive T follicular helper and germinal-centre B cell responses, giving antibodies and T cells.
- C5: Vaccine mRNA and spike protein can persist in lymph node germinal centres for weeks (detected up to day 60 after dose 2), longer than "a few days".
- C6: In the pivotal BNT162b2 trial, two doses were 95% effective against symptomatic COVID-19 from 7 days after dose 2 over a median ~2 months of follow-up.
- C7: Protection against infection and symptomatic disease wanes by ~20–30 percentage points over 6 months, while protection against severe disease stays mostly high.
- C8: mRNA vaccines are non-integrating: the mRNA acts in the cytoplasm and is degraded; changing DNA would need reverse transcription, nuclear entry, and integration.
- C9: The one in-vitro report of reverse transcription (Aldén 2022, Huh7 liver cancer cells) did not show integration, and its model and doses have been criticised as not reflecting vaccinated people.
