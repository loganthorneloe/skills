- claim: C5, C8 (support, illustrative)

- source: The transmission mechanism of monetary policy — https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/1999/q2/the-transmission-mechanism-of-monetary-policy

- asset: assets/boe1999-chart1-gdp.png, assets/boe1999-chart2-inflation.png (vector page region rendered at 220 dpi with titles and notes)
- inspected: opened both crops; titles, axes 1–12 quarters, value scales, notes all inside; no clipping

- limits: Two model runs with different policy rules, not a confidence band or forecast; 1999 UK economy; axis numbers lack minus signs (below-the-line values are negative, marked by +/−).
- rights: Bank of England copyright; same terms as E1. Embedded with attribution.

### E3: Estimated UK responses to a surprise 1pp Bank Rate rise, 1997–2019
- claim: C5, C8, C1 (support)
- type: figure
- source: About a rate of (general) interest — as E1
- locator: Chart 4, PDF p.27; text p.26; Table A p.28
- asset: assets/boe2024-chart4-irfs.png (page region rendered at 150 dpi, 1049×1488; the embedded JPEG alone clips the right-hand axis labels, which the PDF draws outside it)
- inspected: opened at full size; title, eight panels, both axes incl. right-hand values, months 0–36, bands visible

### E4: US price level after a 1pp monetary shock, 1969–1996
- claim: C5 (support), C8 (qualify)
- type: figure+metric
- source: A New Measure of Monetary Shocks — https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/0002828042002651 (author copy https://eml.berkeley.edu/~dromer/papers/AER_September04.pdf)
- locator: Figure 4, AER p.1075; text p.1073
- asset: assets/romer2004-fig4-price-level.png (page region rendered at 300 dpi without annotations; the native embedded image is stored inverted, so the page render is used to preserve appearance). Metric: PPI finished goods "virtually zero for the first 22 months"; −1.9% after 30 months; −5.9% after 48 months; 2-SE interval at 48 months (−8.0%, −3.7%).
- inspected: opened crop; axis, solid estimate and dotted 1-SE bands visible; the source's own blue frame partly overlaps the x-axis title and the "48" tick in the original PDF
- notice: The line hugs zero for nearly two years, then falls steadily; the drag on prices arrives long after the rate change.
- limits: Producer prices, not consumer; magnitude disputed by Coibion (2012); bands are one standard error.
- rights: rights unstated on author copy (AEA holds copyright of journal version). Embedded with attribution, caption says rights unstated.

### E5: Average months until prices bottom out, by country
- claim: C7 (support)
- type: table+metric
- source: Transmission Lags of Monetary Policy: A Meta-Analysis — https://www.ijcb.org/journal/v9n4/transmission-lags-monetary-policy-meta-analysis
- locator: Table 3, p.46; abstract p.39; Table 2 p.45
- asset: assets/havranek2013-table3.png; transcribed: US 42.2; Euro Area 48.4; Japan 51.3; Germany 33.4; UK 40.4; France 51.3; Italy 26.6; Poland 18.7; Czech Republic 14.8; Hungary 17.9; Slovakia 10.7; Slovenia 17.6 (months). Metric: corrected average 29 months; raw mean 33.5 (SD 19.4, n=198); hump-shaped mean 18.2, strictly decreasing 49.1.
- inspected: crop opened; all 12 rows and notes visible; values transcribed and checked against text extraction
- notice: Developed economies cluster around 3–4 years; less financially developed post-transition economies around 1–1.5 years. Same tool, very different delays.
- limits: Country averages from all impulse responses, including strictly decreasing ones whose "lag" is the last horizon reported, which pushes the averages up.
- rights: rights unstated (page shows only a copyright line). Rebuilt as HTML table with exact values and attribution.
