# Evidence records

### E1: Transmission mechanism diagram
- claim: C1, C2, C3 (support)
- type: figure
- source: The transmission mechanism of monetary policy (Bank of England, 1999) — https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/quarterly-bulletin/1999/the-transmission-mechanism-of-monetary-policy.pdf
- locator: p.161, unnumbered figure "The transmission mechanism of monetary policy"
- asset: ../assets/boe1999-transmission-diagram.png (rendered from PDF vector page at 220 dpi, 1357×679)
- inspected: opened at full size; all boxes, arrows, title and note visible, no clipping
- notice: The official rate fans out to four things at once (market rates, asset prices, expectations, exchange rate), which converge on demand; only demand relative to capacity and import prices reach inflation. The rate never touches prices directly.
- limits: The source's note says not all interactions are shown.
- rights: Bank of England copyright; personal/non-commercial display permitted, re-use typically granted for educational use on request (bankofengland.co.uk/legal). Embedded with attribution.

### E2: Simulated GDP and inflation response to a 1-point rise held one year
- claim: C5 (support), C6 (context)
- type: figure+metric
- source: Bank of England 1999, as above
- locator: p.170, Charts 1 and 2; text p.162 and p.170
- asset: ../assets/boe1999-chart1-gdp.png; ../assets/boe1999-chart2-inflation.png. Metrics: GDP max fall 0.2%–0.35% after about five quarters; inflation little changed in year one, max fall about 0.2–0.4 percentage points after about nine quarters, still not back to base after three years though policy reversed after one year.
- inspected: both opened at full size; titles, axes, quarter ticks, notes visible
- notice: Output turns down almost at once and bottoms around quarter 5; inflation is flat (even slightly up in one simulation) for about five quarters and only then drops. The gap between the two troughs is the lag between demand and prices.
- limits: Two simulations of one 1999 macro model; not forecasts; the shaded band is not a probability range; cannot be used to infer how much rates must change for a given fall in inflation.
- rights: as E1

### E3: Distribution of estimated lags across 198 studies' responses
- claim: C5 (support, qualify)
- type: figure+table
- source: Havranek & Rusnak, Transmission Lags of Monetary Policy: A Meta-Analysis — https://www.ijcb.org/sites/default/files/journal/v9n4/ijcb-v9n4-transmission-lags-monetary-policy-meta-analysis.pdf
- locator: p.45, Figure 2 and Table 2
- asset: ../assets/havranek2013-fig2-table2-lags.png. Table 2 transcribed: all responses n=198, mean 33.5, median 37, sd 19.4, min 1, max 60 months; hump-shaped n=100, mean 18.2, median 15, sd 14.1, min 1, max 57; strictly decreasing n=98, mean 49.1, median 48, sd 8.6, min 24, max 60. Abstract: corrected average 29 months; max price fall 0.9% after a 1-point hike; developed economies 25–50 months.
- inspected: opened at full size; density curve, three vertical lines, axis labels, notes, all table rows visible
- notice: There is no single lag: estimates spread from a few months to five years, with two clusters depending on the shape of the estimated response. "About two years" sits in the middle of a wide spread.
- limits: Measures the month when the price level bottoms, not inflation; mostly pre-2013 VAR studies; monthly-data studies give shorter lags.
- rights: rights unstated on the PDF; embedded with attribution.

### E4: Quote — prices are reset infrequently
- claim: C6 (support)
- type: quote+metric
- source: Nakamura & Steinsson, Five Facts About Prices (QJE 2008) — https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3556/fivefacts.pdf
- locator: p.1416, introduction
- asset: "these frequency estimates imply uncensored durations of regular prices of between 8 and 11 months." Metric: median 9–12% of identical US consumer items change their non-sale price each month (1988–2005 CPI micro-data); 19–20% including sales.
- inspected: not applicable (text verified in extracted PDF text)
- notice: A typical shelf price stays put most of a year, so even when demand weakens, the measured price index can only drift down as individual prices come up for review.
- limits: US, 1988–2005; counting temporary sales halves the durations; frequency of increases rises with inflation.
- rights: quotation, fair use length

### E5: UK mortgagors' time shielded from rate changes
- claim: C6 (support), C5 (context for currentness)
- type: figure
- source: Cumming, Bitesize: Fixing ideas (Bank Underground, 2019) — https://bankunderground.co.uk/2019/09/27/bitesize-fixing-ideas-the-slowing-of-interest-rate-pass-through-to-mortgagors/
- locator: Chart 1
- asset: ../assets/bankunderground2019-chart1-wait.jpg (original 665×567 image file)
- inspected: opened at natural size; axis, legend, note, source line visible
- notice: The average time until a UK mortgage reverts to a variable rate rose from about a year before 2008 to about 3.5 years by 2019, so a rate rise reaches most mortgage payments only gradually as fixes expire.
- limits: UK to 2019; chart note refers to the stock of mortgages while text says new mortgagors; staff blog views, not Bank policy.
- rights: Bank of England copyright (as E1); embedded with attribution.
