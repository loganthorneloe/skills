## Transfer probe: write skew and why the isolation name is insufficient

**From A alone:** Suppose two warehouse supervisors can each disable one of two backup workers, provided at least one remains. Both count two on a stable snapshot, then update different workers. Row-write collision checks cannot stop the pair; the result violates the rule and cannot match either serial order. A stable snapshot is therefore insufficient. In PostgreSQL 18, Repeatable Read excludes phantoms yet still permits such nonserial outcomes. Put the check and dependent update in one Serializable transaction and retry the complete decision on 40001, or design locks covering the shared dependency and every participating path. Locking only each worker’s own row fails. A level name alone does not specify another database’s implementation, transaction boundaries, or whether all dependent operations use compatible protection. Grounding: “A stable view can still produce an invalid decision,” “Three practical ways,” and the table’s PostgreSQL qualifications.

**From B alone:** The same backup-worker example reproduces §4: both decisions omit the other’s concurrent change, and different writes avoid a direct collision. Serializable must prevent both unsafe commits; a fresh retry sees the remaining worker and refuses the second removal. Under an explicit locking design, acquire the common protection before reading and computing, not just before writing an already calculated answer. A lost-counter update has the narrower repair `SET sold = sold + 1` at PostgreSQL Read Committed, because it uses the updated row; that repair does not establish safety for multirow rules. Inspect the database/version semantics, complete transaction boundaries, and participating paths rather than trusting a name. Grounding: §§2–6.

## Primary-source verification

- [PostgreSQL 18 isolation reference](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/transaction-iso.html), Table 13.1 and §§13.2.1–13.2.3, verifies the table cells, own-write visibility, statement versus transaction snapshots, conflicting-update behavior, and Serializable guarantee. Its Read Committed update semantics support both absolute-assignment failures and relative-update repairs; A’s condition is reevaluated on the updated row.
- [Ports and Grittner, Figure 1 and §2.1.1](https://drkp.net/papers/ssi-vldb12.pdf#page=2) confirms both reads precede the independent updates, both snapshot-isolation transactions can commit, and neither sequential ordering removes both doctors. The reports appropriately avoid treating the paper’s old performance numbers as current predictions.
- [Failure handling §13.5](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/mvcc-serialization-failure-handling.html) verifies SQLSTATE 40001, complete retries including application logic, and lack of a guarantee that the next attempt succeeds.
- [Locking §§13.3.2 and 13.3.4](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/explicit-locking.html) verifies row-writer waiting, ordinary reads remaining possible, transaction-end release, deadlock detection, and ordering locks consistently.
- [Constraints §5.5](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/ddl-constraints.html) supports the cross-row CHECK limitation. B correctly includes NOT NULL when claiming to reject missing counts. A only claims that its CHECK rejects negative values, which is also correct.
- [Transaction tutorial](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/tutorial-transactions.html) and [MVCC introduction](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/mvcc-intro.html) support the foundational explanations. Their broad introductory claims are appropriately bounded by the reports’ later implementation-specific discussion.

No live database experiment was performed for this evaluation. SQL semantics were checked against the cited versioned manual; examples are not measured traces. Browser inspection included desktop/mobile renderings, image alternatives, scroll containers, and the wide-table/figure regions, not a comprehensive assistive-technology audit.

**Major issues:** none. Conceptual-score confidence: high; overall one-point winner confidence: modest.
