      sensitivity — What does a derivative mean without assuming calculus?

      supported

      relu_rule — How does ReLU pass a tiny change?

      supported

      For z>0 ReLU has sensitivity 1; for z<0 it has sensitivity 0. At z=0 left and right change ratios disagree, so no ordinary derivative exists. Our example stays away from zero.

      Evidence

      • url

      reverse — How does backpropagation obtain every parameter sensitivity efficiently?

      supported

      Start the loss sensitivity at 1. Visit recorded operations in reverse dependency order. Multiply incoming sensitivity by each local sensitivity, and add to each input’s accumulated sensitivity. Saved forward values supply the local rules. This reuses downstream work across parameters.

      update — How can a gradient change the parameters to lower loss?

      supported

      Choose a positive learning rate eta and set parameter_i_new = parameter_i - eta*g_i, where g_i is that parameter’s present loss sensitivity. The predicted tiny-step loss change is sum(g_i*(-eta*g_i)) = -eta*sum(g_i*g_i), negative if any g_i is nonzero. This is local reasoning, so check the actual recomputed loss after a finite step.

      training — How does this calculation become learning over examples?

      supported

      Repeat prediction, loss, backward calculation, and parameter update. For a mean loss over examples, average their gradient contributions. A sampled mini-batch approximates the full-data gradient. Recompute after updates because the current values and sensitivities change.

      target — How does backpropagation tell a small network how to adjust each weight to learn?

      supported

      It computes how a tiny change of each parameter would change the present scalar loss, by composing local arithmetic sensitivities backward through the forward calculation. An optimizer uses those sensitivities to update parameters, and training repeats this across examples.

      why

      why
      This network’s forward evaluation includes ReLU; omitting its rule leaves predictions undetermined.

      why
      Loss cannot quantify the requested mismatch without a desired output.

      why
      Loss compares the actual computed prediction with that desired output.

      why

      why
      The other-input factor must be interpreted as a local change ratio.

      why
      The factors 0 and 1 describe local change ratios.

      activation → relu_rule

      why
      ReLU’s piecewise function must be known to determine its local response.

      sensitivity → chain

      why
      Multiplying change ratios is unintelligible without defining those ratios.

      add_rule → reverse

      why
      Backward propagation through weighted addition needs its local rule.

      multiply_rule → reverse

      why
      Backward propagation through weighting and squared error needs its product rule.

      relu_rule → reverse

      why
      This chosen network includes a ReLU requiring its backward rule.

      accumulate → reverse

      why
      Repeated uses require summing returned contributions, including the error used twice in its square.

      loss → reverse

      why
      A scalar objective anchors what all returned sensitivities refer to.

      gradient → update

      why
      Update direction and amount use each parameter’s sensitivity.

      update → training

      why
      Learning requires an actual parameter change between successive calculations.

      The answer needs the mechanism that computes parameter sensitivities.

      training → target

      why
      The answer needs the repeated update procedure that uses those sensitivities.

      arithmetic → activation

      The max comparison and zero define this piecewise operation.

      sensitivity → accumulate

      why
      Returned effects must be understood as small-change sensitivities before their sum has meaning.

      loss → gradient

      why
      A loss gradient must identify which scalar objective its components refer to.

      sensitivity → gradient

      why
      The collection consists of individual partial sensitivities.

      chain → reverse
