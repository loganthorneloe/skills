# Editing Standards and Voice Preservation Rubric

Use this reference to guide inline edits and preserve author voice across passages and full articles.

## Core Editing Dimensions

### 1. Grammar and Mechanics
* Subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, dangling modifiers, and pronoun referents.
* Punctuation clarity without introducing prohibited punctuation like em dashes or parenthetical asides.
* Spelling errors, typos, and repeated words.
* In technical writing, preserve exact literals, variable names, CLI commands, code blocks, and API signatures.

### 2. Clarity and Directness
* Remove accidental syntactic ambiguity where a sentence can be interpreted in two conflicting ways.
* Eliminate wordy constructions and redundant phrases when a simpler phrasing improves readability.
* Do not simplify technical concepts, flatten technical vocabulary, or remove necessary domain terminology.
* Do not add explanatory bridges or introductory announcements.

### 3. Voice and Tone Preservation
* Preserve the author's direct, peer-to-peer engineering tone.
* Never inject academic hedging, corporate filler, or synthetic transitions.
* Never smooth out unique sentence rhythms or stylistic cadence.
* Never alter the underlying claims, opinions, or conclusions expressed by the author.

## Distinction Between Edits and Author Decisions

* Direct Inline Edits. Clear grammatical errors, typos, misspellings, punctuation slips, and unambiguous syntactic tangles. These are corrected directly inline.
* Author Decisions. Material ambiguities where fixing the text requires guessing the author's technical intent, apparent contradictions in data or claims, or missing arguments. These are not silently modified; they are flagged in the change report for the author to resolve.

## Change Report Output Standards

Every inline edit must be accounted for in the change report.
* Location or original excerpt
* What was modified
* Concrete reason why the change was made in short bullets
